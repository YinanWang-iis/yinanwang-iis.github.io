Research

My research combines first-principles calculations and machine learning to understand core-loss spectra and the atomic-scale dynamics of two-dimensional ferroelectric materials.

Core-loss spectroscopy

I study how core-loss spectra encode local electronic structure, and how to make their theoretical description more consistent with experiment. My work follows two connected directions: interpreting spectra through machine learning, and correcting systematic discrepancies in excited-supercell core-hole (XCH) calculations.

Learning from physically meaningful energy windows

Using doped graphene as a model system, I apply region-resolved machine learning to X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) spectra. I examine how different physical energy windows contribute to model learning and provide physical explanations for their differing predictive performance. This connects the information learned by a model with the underlying spectral features and dopant-induced changes in electronic structure.

Bringing XCH calculations closer to experiment

Two systematic discrepancies motivate my correction work: underestimated separations between absorption peaks, and underestimated absolute transition energies, which also affect chemical shifts. I address these problems with complementary approaches.

Correcting absorption-peak separations with machine learning

XCH calculations can underestimate the energy separations between absorption peaks, producing a systematic mismatch with experimental core-loss spectra. I use machine learning to correct this discrepancy and improve agreement between simulated and measured spectra. This work focuses on the relative energy scale within a spectrum, complementing the absolute-energy correction below.

Related paper

Correcting absolute transition energies with a single-atom reference

Fixed-reference PAW-XCH calculations can underestimate absolute transition energies and, consequently, chemical shifts. I address this using an all-electron single-atom reference based on core-electron total-energy calculations. This approach targets the absolute energy scale and the differences between chemical environments, supporting more consistent comparisons between calculated and experimental core-loss spectra.

Related paper

2D ferroelectric materials

My research explores sliding ferroelectricity in bilayer hexagonal boron nitride. I use deep-learning interatomic potentials and molecular dynamics to investigate how relative motion between the layers is connected to polarization reversal.

I am particularly interested in the atomic-scale dynamics of switching and its electromechanical response, including both single-domain and domain-wall configurations. These simulations help connect local atomic motion with the mechanisms of ferroelectric switching in two-dimensional materials.
